# SC04 finite language contract

This contract defines an experiment, not Miter's universal language, voice, or purposes. All sentence-level semantics are computed by native MeTTa. The lexical membrane only lowercases words, splits punctuation and retains exact zero-based, end-exclusive Unicode-codepoint offsets. One sentence is one scoped raw contact; final punctuation is optional. Unrecognized wording remains unresolved.

The following grammar productions compose independently declared names and interface roles. `A` is an artifact name, `P` a person name, `V` is `choose`, `inspect`, or `restore`. `VS` is one or more V separated by `and`.

- `please revise A` and `A needs revision`: a requested revised artifact relation.
- `do not revise A`: prohibition of that exact relation, not an inferred lack of ability.
- `keep P able to VS A`, `P must be able to VS A`, and `P must still be able to VS A`: obligations to retain those participation interfaces during the requested artifact change.
- `P may VS A`: permission, not an obligation, proof of present ability, or cancellation of a prior commitment.
- `P no longer needs to VS A`: an explicit release of the corresponding finite participation obligation when supported by the admitted human source.

Names and the role-to-interface endpoints are human-declared meaning premises in the laboratory registry. They do not establish actual availability. Independently observed graph relations establish current availability. Multiple conflicting name meanings remain ambiguous; repeated equivalent meaning declarations add no new meaning.

The raw contact envelope declares the referenced artifact, just as a scoped project/document surface can identify its object. A parsed name must agree with that object. Unknown wording on a different declared object does not automatically veto the selected artifact's work; uncertainty on the selected object remains material until resolved. The experiment does not claim that arbitrary prose can always be assigned a non-material topic.

An interpretation preserves raw origin, source span, syntax derivation, name/interface proof dependencies and inherited root identities. A model suggestion cannot create a raw observation, permission, release or authoritative meaning. Matching proposals retain model provenance and add no independent root; unsupported suggestions remain candidates for independent clarification, not executable code.

Training and generated unfamiliar cases share this declared domain. Only concrete generated instances revealed after the implementation/generator/checker freeze may be called held-out, and only within this finite grammar. No externally blinded semantic evaluation is claimed.
